# RAY FINDS HIS PLACE — Storyboard & Production Script

**Working title:** Ray Finds His Place
**Scenario id (proposed):** `ray-finds-his-place`
**Runtime:** ~4 min 40 s of story (every path lands between 4:38 and 4:46) + 2 polls at ~60 s each ≈ 6¾ min wall clock
**Audience:** Adults, cozy-funny. Clean enough for any room, but the jokes are pitched at grown-ups.
**Shots:** 32 on any single path · 50 in the whole document
**Fiction notice:** None needed. Nothing here resembles a real organisation, event or person.

**The question the room argues about afterwards:** When someone doesn't want you around yet, do you win them over by being quiet and patient, or by being loudly, joyfully yourself? And did Belle soften because of *what* Ray did, or just because he kept showing up?

**Where the votes go:** Each poll sends Ray down a visibly different path (a slow, silent approach vs. a full comic performance), and each vote adds one point to a hidden score, either `snuggles` or `dances`. The two scores decide which of three endings plays: two snuggle votes give a cozy ending, two dance votes give a kitchen dance party, and a split gives a mixed ending. The first poll's branches rejoin at the storm about 50 seconds later, because the weather comes no matter what Ray did that afternoon. The first vote still counts, because it is added to the score that picks the ending.

**A note on voices:** Ray and Belle talk only to each other and to the audience. The Human never hears them, and nothing the Human says answers what a cat said.

## Visual style

This is a picture book that happens to move. Every frame is a **warm painterly storybook illustration in gouache**, with soft coloured-pencil texture on top and visible paper grain underneath. Shapes are rounded and gentle, and the mood is cozy with a little comic tilt. Nothing is photoreal.

**Palette:** cream, oatmeal, butter yellow, sage green and dusty rose, with deep plum in the shadows. Belle is the only true black in the film, which keeps her readable against any background. The storm act (Act D and its branches) shifts to cool slate blue with a single warm lamp, then the endings return to morning gold or evening rose.

**Light:** soft and low-contrast, with glowing highlights. Daylight scenes come through a big window, and night scenes are lit by storm light plus one lamp.

**Lens:** the camera lives at **cat eye level**, about a foot off the floor. A 35 mm feel with shallow depth of field makes furniture look monumental and people vanish above the knee. That framing is the whole joke of the house.

**Continuity locks:** Ray is always a tiny, very fluffy, pure-white ragamuffin kitten with a plush ruff, a plume tail and big round sky-blue eyes. Belle is always a lean, glossy, all-black adult cat with amber eyes and a notched left ear. The Human is only ever grey wool slippers, oatmeal trousers from the knee down, or hands and forearms in sage knit sleeves.

**Never:** text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, signatures, brand names, visible screens, speech bubbles, collars or tags, clothing on the animals, human faces.

The style paragraph is written out in full at the end of every IMAGE prompt below, word for word.
